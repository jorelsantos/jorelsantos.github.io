2026–Present

Research Discovery Knowledge Graph

An AI-grounded search tool that helps social-science instructors find datasets by how they've actually been used.

Python · Neo4j · semantic search · Next.js · LLM grounding

ContextInstructors and researchers can't easily find datasets across ICPSR, OpenAlex, and DataCite — there's no shared discovery layer, and keyword search can't show how a dataset has actually been used. Built at the Institute for Social Research under Prof. Libby Hemphill.

What I didSole builder. I built a Neo4j knowledge graph — 349K nodes, 2M+ relationships — linking datasets to the publications that cite them, added semantic search and a graph API, and shipped a Next.js app on Vercel. Midway, user testing showed our audience was too broad, so I drove a pivot to a focused instructor segment and rewrote the product brief and 15-week roadmap around it.

ResultsA deployed tool that ranks datasets by citation evidence and shows the citing papers as proof. The pivot refocused the roadmap on a user we could actually serve, once testing exposed the gap between team assumptions and what the data could support.

2025–Present

Rabbit Hole

Find the shortest collaboration path between any two artists — the discovery feature streaming platforms don't have.

Python · Neo4j · graph algorithms · Spotify data

ContextStreaming platforms recommend by genre and popularity, not by how artists connect through collaborations. I wanted to trace the path between any two musicians — who features on whose tracks — and turn it into a way to discover music.

What I didSolo builder. I engineered a 119,729-artist collaboration graph from a 30GB archive to replace the rate-limited Spotify API. Before scaling, I ran feasibility spikes — a 67-song preview test that auto-resolved 79% and played back 100% — and gated an 8x graph expansion on accuracy and size guardrails before promoting to production.

ResultsA working shortest-path discovery prototype over 119K artists, on architecture I validated before committing to it instead of after.

Rebuilt a capability the team lost when its vendor tool sunset — self-service Salesforce auditing across 350+ objects.

Salesforce (API/SOQL) · SQL · Python · Snowflake

ContextWhen the vendor platform Cuneiform sunset, the Center for Academic Innovation lost the ability to audit how its Salesforce instance was used — just as it was weighing a Snowflake migration. Revenue data was also scattered across Coursera, edX, and Salesforce with no shared view.

What I didI built a self-service tool that lets any non-technical CAI team audit Salesforce utilization across 350+ objects, turning a lost capability into a repeatable input for the migration decision. Then I unified the fragmented revenue data into one standardized model.

Apr 2026

Where Do UMSI Students Land?

8 years of career placement data, mined for what actually gets students hired.

Python · pandas · hypothesis testing · clustering

ContextUMSI's Career Development Office had 8 years of placement survey data (2017–2024) in two messy, separate files. Nobody had unified it to ask what actually predicts a good outcome. Team project with Tyler Tran and Kenny Wang.

What I didI merged and cleaned 1,374 placement records, standardized organization names, and engineered a search-strategy variable from free-text survey responses. Then ran chi-square tests, a regression on strategy over time, and k-means and agglomerative clustering.

ResultsNetworking drove 25.7% of placements — more than any other method, and the gap was significant (χ²(5) = 361.25, p < 0.001). COVID accelerated the pattern rather than reversing it (χ²(5) = 29.80, p < 0.001). A few organizations — Michigan, Google, and Amazon — took a disproportionate share of the pipeline.

Dec 2025

NBA Players Are Getting Leaner, Faster

A normalized SQL database of ~3,000 NBA players, built to test a hunch about how player builds are changing.

SQL · relational database design · DataGrip · ERD

ContextI started with a raw dataset of ~3,000 NBA players and wanted to design a proper relational database around it, not just query a flat file — then use it to test whether builds have shifted as the league got more athletic.

What I didI designed an ERD and built five normalized tables — players, schools, teams, positions, and countries — choosing weight and years of experience as key fields and deliberately skipping lookup tables the data didn't justify.

ResultsPlayers with fewer than 6 years of experience average 206 lbs, versus 217 lbs for players with 6+ years — incoming players trend leaner. Separately, 317 of the ~3,000 players traced back to Big Ten schools.
