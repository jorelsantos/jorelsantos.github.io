Where Do UMSI Students Land?

8 years of career placement data, mined for what actually gets students hired.

Python · pandas · hypothesis testing · clustering

Context. UMSI's Career Development Office had 8 years of internship and full-time placement survey data (2017-2024) sitting in two separate, messy files — nobody had unified it to ask what actually predicts a good outcome. Team project with Tyler Tran and Kenny Wang.

What I did. Merged and cleaned 1,374 placement records into one dataset, standardized organization names, and engineered a search-strategy category from free-text survey responses. Ran chi-square tests on career stage vs. strategy and on pre/post-COVID strategy shifts, a regression on strategy growth over time, and k-means/agglomerative clustering to find distinct placement profiles.

Results. Networking drove 25.7% of all placements — more than any other method, and the gap was statistically significant (χ²(5) = 361.25, p < 0.001). COVID didn't reverse that pattern, it accelerated it (χ²(5) = 29.80, p < 0.001): networking's share of placements grew after COVID, not despite it. A small number of organizations, led by the University of Michigan, Google, and Amazon, account for a disproportionate share of the pipeline.

What I learned.

What excites me. Turning a messy institutional dataset into findings the Career Development Office could actually act on — different advice for internship vs. full-time seekers, and where to spend workshop time.
